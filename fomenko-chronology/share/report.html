1. The short version

Fomenko and Nosovsky say most of “ancient” history is medieval history copied backward. I put ten of their cases on one axis, 3100 BCE to 2026 CE. Blue is the accepted date. Orange is theirs. Every gap is counted without a year 0.

Eight of the ten have a number. Their median move is about 1,800 years later. The biggest is the Great Pyramid: Khufu’s reign, c. 2589–2566 BCE, moved into a 14th–16th-century build era. That is +3,866 to +4,188 years. The smallest is Rome, Sulla to Caracalla, moved onto the Holy Roman Empire: +1,037 to +1,046 years. Egypt and Classical Greece don’t get a number. They are placed as whole eras inside the 11th–16th centuries.

2. What kind of date it is

The two columns don’t always measure the same thing, and the chart keeps them apart.

  • A zodiac reading is a date, not a reign. Seti I’s 969 and 1206 CE are two answers to one horoscope. Neither is a period he ruled.
  • A reign isn’t a build date. Khufu’s reign is set against their era for large pyramids, 14th–16th c. Chron4 ch.20 also says “X–XI century the earliest,” with some “as late as the XVII.”
  • An era isn’t a shift. Egypt, c. 3100–30 BCE, is about 3,070 years long. They fit it inside about 600, so there is no single offset.
  • The crucifixion date moved twice. Chron4 ch.20 says 1095. Their later revision (Tsar of the Slavs, 2004) says birth 1152 and crucifixion 1185. This sheet uses the revision and flags the older one.

3. How the shifts work

There are three offset families. Each one is a different distance from the same origin. You don’t add them. Chron1 ch.6 §7 says it plainly: “All the three shifts are counted off the same point.”

Right: copy = original − 333/360, or − 1,053, or − 1,778…1,810. Chron2 ch.3 names them the Roman–Byzantine, Roman and Graeco-Biblical shifts.

Wrong: 333 + 1,053 + 1,778 = 3,164. Nobody proposes that number. There is also no universal 600-year correction anywhere in the sources.

Gaps measured between two copies, not from the origin, can equal a difference or a sum. Fig. 6.20 labels Example 6 “720 = 1053 – 333.” Table 2 calls Example 2’s ≈1,300 “the sum of two basic shifts.”

5. Where the pages disagree with themselves

  1. Ex. 1: the text says ≈333 and the caption says ≈330–360.
  2. Ex. 7 has three numbers for one pair: ≈362 in the text, ≈360 in the captions, and a mean reign-end shift of 373.
  3. Ex. 9: “911 AD = 928 BC … 1839.” Counted without a year 0, that is 1,838. Fig. 6.26 says ≈1,830.
  4. Ex. 12: “970 AD = 911 AD … 60 year shift.” The difference is 59.
  5. Ex. 17: the text gives 210–270 forward. Fig. 6.47 prints ≈275.
  6. Parthenon: “447 b.c. … shift of 1810 years … 1363 a.d.” With no year 0, the arithmetic gives 1364.
  7. Ex. 5 ends in 887 in the text and 888 in the captions. Byzantium II is 829–1204 in Ex. 13 and 867–1143 in Ex. 14.
  8. Fig. 6.50 prints one Spartan reign as “330-397” BC.
  9. No offset is printed for Examples 3, 4, 18 and 20.

6. What this sheet is not

  • It is not a redated list of every pharaoh. Egypt is shown as one era.
  • It applies no universal correction. None exists in the sources.
  • It doesn’t endorse the claim. Historians, Egyptologists and astronomers reject the New Chronology. This sheet only reports it accurately.
